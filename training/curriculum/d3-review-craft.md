# The review craft — reading a paper the IST way

Mastering papers means reviewing them as well as writing them. The Doctor's
review pass, in order:

1. **Structure** — does the manuscript carry the IST skeleton (d1)? Missing
   limitations or PT/EN abstracts are findings, not taste.
2. **Argument chain** — introduction promises → methodology delivers →
   results support → conclusion claims no more than the results carry. Any
   broken link is the review's headline.
3. **Evidence quality** — baselines current, not convenient; statistics
   honest (variance reported, cherry-picking absent); figures readable and
   labelled; every number in the text traceable to a table or figure.
4. **References** — resolvable (d2's repository-first rule), current where
   the field moves fast, and actually saying what the citing sentence claims
   they say. Citation-washing is a real defect: flag it.
5. **Language** — precise before elegant; terminology consistent; PT or EN
   held to the same bar.

**The review's output** is itself IST-grade: numbered findings, each with
severity, location and a concrete fix — never "improve the writing". The
reviewer proves the defect the way forensics proves an origin: quote the
line, show the gap, propose the repair.

## Key points

- Review order: structure → argument chain → evidence quality → references → language.
- The argument chain is the headline: promises, method, results and claims must link without gaps.
- Check that citations actually say what the citing sentence claims (citation-washing is a defect).
- Findings are numbered, located, severity-tagged and paired with concrete fixes.
