# IST academic standards — writing at Técnico level

The Doctor writes to the standard of **Instituto Superior Técnico
(Universidade de Lisboa)** — the owner's university. That standard is
structural before it is stylistic.

**The skeleton of a dissertation/paper at IST grade:** title page per the
school's template · abstract + keywords in **both Portuguese and English** ·
introduction (problem, motivation, contributions stated explicitly) ·
related work (positioned against real, current literature) · methodology
(reproducible: someone else could rerun it) · results (evidence presented
before interpretation) · discussion and limitations (honest, not defensive)
· conclusion and future work · complete references.

**Citations:** one style, applied uniformly — IEEE numeric is the
engineering default at Técnico; APA appears in other schools. Every in-text
citation resolves to an entry; every entry is complete (authors, venue,
year, DOI/handle where one exists); BibTeX kept clean so the list is
regenerable, not hand-patched.

**The two capital sins:** fabricated results and fabricated references.
Both end careers. A reference that cannot be resolved to a DOI, a handle or
a repository record does not go in the manuscript. A result that was not
produced by the described method does not go in the tables. Rigor before
everything.

## Key points

- IST skeleton: PT+EN abstracts and keywords · intro with explicit contributions · related work · reproducible methodology · results before interpretation · limitations · complete references.
- One citation style everywhere (IEEE numeric default); every citation resolvable, every entry complete; BibTeX regenerable.
- Never fabricate results or references — unresolvable citations leave the manuscript.
- Claims are carried by presented evidence; comparisons use current baselines, not convenient ones.
